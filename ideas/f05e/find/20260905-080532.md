### Executive Summary

Importing cosmetics into the United States requires navigating strict regulatory boundaries between cosmetics and drugs under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) and the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA). 

* **Cosmetics vs. Drugs:** The FDA regulates cosmetics based on their "intended use," which is derived from marketing claims, product labeling, and ingredients. Claims that imply treating, curing, or preventing disease, or altering the structure/function of the body (e.g., anti-wrinkle, acne treatment, SPF), classify a product as a drug. Dual-classified products (e.g., anti-dandruff shampoo, SPF moisturizer) must satisfy both cosmetic and drug frameworks.
* **Import Refusals & Alerts (India Focus):** Products with unapproved drug claims or unauthorized color additives face Detention Without Physical Examination (DWPE) and import refusals under FD&C Act Section 801(a). Import Alert 66-41 lists numerous Indian products detained for unapproved drug status, including soaps, creams, toothpastes, and herbal formulations.
* **Importer of Record (IOR) & MoCRA Mandates:** As of July 1, 2024, cosmetic manufacturing facilities must be registered with the FDA, and products must be listed using the Structured Product Labeling (SPL) framework via *Cosmetics Direct*. Importers of Record must secure a U.S. Customs bond and coordinate entry data with U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP).
* **Prior Notice Rules:** Prior Notice under 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart I (Section 801(m)) applies statutorily to food, beverages, and dietary supplements. While some logistics blogs mention Prior Notice alongside cosmetics, statutory and FDA regulatory texts confirm standard cosmetics undergo electronic entry screening and Partner Government Agency (PGA) data validation rather than standard food Prior Notice.

### Cosmetic vs. Drug Claims: Regulatory Thresholds & Enforcement

The FDA defines cosmetics as products intended to cleanse, beautify, promote attractiveness, or alter appearance. Drugs are defined as articles intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, or intended to affect the structure or function of the body.

#### Key Determinants of Drug Classification:
* **Explicit Claims:** Label wording such as "cures skin redness," "treats acne," "repairs damaged skin," or "eliminates wrinkles" pushes a cosmetic into the drug regulatory framework. 
* **Label Formatting:** Simply designating cosmetic ingredients under the heading "Active Ingredients" has triggered FDA import detentions, as the agency associates that phrase with pharmaceutical products.
* **Marketing Channels Scrutinized:** Intended use is evaluated across consumer-facing touchpoints, including company websites, social media channels, promotional literature, and online marketplaces—not just physical packaging.
* **Dual-Category Products:** Formulations offering cosmetic and therapeutic benefits (e.g., anti-dandruff shampoos, sunscreens/SPF moisturizers) must simultaneously comply with cosmetic rules and Over-The-Counter (OTC) drug monographs or pre-market approvals.

### Import Alerts, Refusals, and Enforcement (India Focus)

Under Section 801(a) of the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C. 381), the FDA enforces an "appearance standard." If a product appears to be adulterated, misbranded, or an unapproved new drug, it can be refused admission.

* **Import Alert 53-06:** Detention Without Physical Examination (DWPE) of cosmetics that are adulterated and/or misbranded due to color additive violations.
* **Import Alert 53-17:** DWPE of cosmetics due to microbiological contamination.
* **Import Alert 53-21:** DWPE of adulterated cosmetic products and ingredients.
* **Import Alert 45-02:** Illegal/undeclared colors in foods and cosmetics.
* **Import Alert 99-34:** DWPE of foreign establishments lacking valid registration/listing.

#### Import Refusal Protocol:
* When detained under DWPE or standard entry review, the Importer of Record must refute the violation (e.g., valid private laboratory audit) or apply for authorization to recondition (e.g., relabeling).
* If the FDA issues a final **Notice of FDA Action (Refusal Notice)**, the shipment must be destroyed or re-exported under CBP supervision within **90 days**. 
* Failure to re-deliver the product within 90 days triggers CBP liquidated damages against the customs bond. The FDA cannot extend this 90-day window.

### Importer of Record (IOR), Customs, and MoCRA Compliance

Cosmetic imports require coordination between U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and FDA entry reviewers. 

```
                                [ US CUSTOMS & BORDER PROTECTION ]
                                                 |
            +------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
            |                                                                         |
            v                                                                         v
   [ Commercial Formal Entry ]                                               [ FDA Compliance Review ]
  - Entry Summary & Commercial Invoice                                      - Verification of FEI & Listings
  - Bill of Lading (within 5 working days)                                  - PGA Data Elements Transmission
  - CBP Form 301 (Customs Bond)                                             - Adulteration/Misbranding Screens
  - 10-digit HTS Classification                                             - MoCRA Adverse Event Framework
  - Duty, MPF, HMF, Section 301
```

#### Core MoCRA & Customs Requirements:
1. **Facility Registration & Product Listing:** Since July 1, 2024, all domestic and foreign manufacturing/processing facilities must register with the FDA to obtain an FDA Establishment Identifier (FEI). Product listings must be filed via *Cosmetics Direct* using Structured Product Labeling (SPL).
   * New cosmetic products must be listed within 120 days of marketing.
   * Product changes must be updated within 60 days; general listings require annual review updates.
2. **Responsible Person & Labeling Mandates:** The product label must state the designated Responsible Person along with domestic contact details (U.S. address, phone, or website) to capture adverse event reporting.
3. **Adverse Event System:** Serious adverse events must be reported to the FDA within 15 business days, and records must be retained for 6 years.
4. **Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP):** MoCRA rules mandated a proposed GMP rule by December 29, 2024, and a final rule by December 29, 2025.
5. **Entry Documentation (CBP):**
   * Commercial entries require formal filing via CBP Form 301 (Customs Bond—continuous bond recommended for regular importers).
   * Entry documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, entry summary) must be filed within 5 working days of arrival.
   * Accurate 10-digit Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) codes, Partner Government Agency (PGA) data elements, Merchandise Processing Fees (MPF), and Harbor Maintenance Fees (HMF, for ocean freight) must be submitted.

### Prior Notice Rules for Cosmetics

There is an operational distinction between Prior Notice under Section 801(m) and standard FDA cosmetic entry transmissions:

* **Statutory Prior Notice (21 CFR Part 1, Subpart I / Section 801(m)):** Established under the Bioterrorism Act of 2002 and FSMA, Prior Notice explicitly governs all human and animal food, beverages, dietary supplements, and food ingredients.
* **Application to Cosmetics:** Certain secondary logistics resources state that "specific cosmetic products" or "certain cosmetics" require FDA Prior Notice. However, FDA primary literature clarifies that Prior Notice rules apply to ingestible items and dietary products. Standard cosmetic imports are cleared through standard electronic entry processing via CBP/PGA mechanisms rather than food Prior Notice interfaces, unless the product is ingestible, classified as a food/dietary supplement, or bears drug claims requiring alternative pre-arrival clearance.
