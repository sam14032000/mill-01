### Direct Answer: Indian Seller Export Compliance

Exporting from India requires compliance across three main pillars: regulatory licensing via the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT), customs clearance documentation, and foreign exchange compliance governed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA). 

At minimum, every physical exporter must obtain an Importer-Exporter Code (IEC), register an Authorized Dealer (AD) Code with customs, generate commercial trade documents (Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Shipping Bill/Bill of Export), and realize foreign payments within stipulated statutory timelines, securing Foreign Inward Remittance Certificates (FIRCs) or electronic Bank Realisation Certificates (e-BRCs). Sector-specific commodities (e.g., food, pharmaceuticals) require additional certifications from Partner Government Agencies (PGAs).

### Key Thematic Clusters from the Search Results

#### 1. Core Licensing and DGFT Compliance
* **Importer-Exporter Code (IEC):** Issued by the DGFT, the IEC is a mandatory 10-digit alphanumeric identifier (tied to the business PAN) required to clear Indian customs, transport international cargo, and legally receive foreign exchange into an Indian bank account. Application is fully digital via the DGFT portal.
* **Annual Renewal Requirement:** IEC holders must update/revalidate their IEC details annually between April and June, even if there are no structural changes to the business. Failure to update results in deactivation.
* **Service Exporter Nuance:** While mandatory for physical goods, IEC is generally not necessary for service exporters unless they are claiming specific export incentives under the Foreign Trade Policy (FTP).
* **Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG) & Schemes:** Procedures require specific applications (e.g., Form ANF 5A) to access capital goods import benefits tied to export obligations.

#### 2. Customs and Core Shipping Documentation
Indian customs clearance mandates standard commercial and transport documentation:
* **Commercial Invoice:** Must contain full product descriptions, values, trade terms, and matching details aligned across all shipping papers.
* **Packing List:** Detailed breakdown of item quantities, gross/net weights, and packaging dimensions.
* **Shipping Bill / Bill of Export:** Filed with Indian Customs (via ICEGATE) to process clearance. For courier-mode shipments, a courier shipping bill applies.
* **Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (BL/AWB):** Proof of receipt and transport issued by the ocean or air carrier.
* **Certificate of Origin:** Validates the manufacturing origin of goods (often needed for preferential tariffs under trade agreements).
* **Goods and Services Tax (GST) Papers:** Documentation supporting exports under Letter of Undertaking (LUT) or with payment of IGST (e.g., GST RFD-01/11, GSTR-3B).

#### 3. Foreign Exchange (FEMA) and Banking Requirements
* **Manner of Receipt & Settlement:** Transactions must strictly follow the *Foreign Exchange Management (Manner of Receipt & Payment) Regulations, 2016*.
* **Payment Realization Timelines:** Under standard guidelines, export proceeds must be realized within stipulated timelines. Authorized Dealer (AD) Category–I banks are permitted to grant extensions on payment periods up to 6 months at a time, irrespective of invoice value, subject to conditions.
* **Settlement Documentation:** Exporters must collect FIRCs or e-BRCs to close export bills against shipping filings, proving inward remittance compliance to customs and tax authorities.

#### 4. E-Commerce and MSME Cross-Border Selling
* **Courier Export Limits:** The government/DGFT has set a value limit of ₹10,00,000 (₹10 Lakh) per consignment for exports made via courier channels.
* **Policy Integration:** Chapter 1 of the Foreign Trade Policy leverages the *Niryat Bandhu Scheme (NBS)* to provide outreach and handholding for e-commerce and MSME cross-border sellers.
* **Regulatory Alignment:** Active discussions and initiatives between the DGFT and RBI focus on further simplifying FEMA guidelines and easing forex compliance specifically tailored to high-volume, low-value e-commerce transactions.

#### 5. Sector-Specific Regulations
* **Food & Edible Products:** Mandates an Export Category license from the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), Phytosanitary Certificates for fresh agriculture/produce, and destination-specific laboratory test reports.
* **Pharmaceuticals:** Shipments require Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) facility compliance, Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (COPP), product registrations in the destination country, and sector-specific documentation (including narcotics certificates where applicable).
* **Dairy Products:** Requires establishment transfer certificates, batch traceability for dry goods, supplier Certificates of Conformance/Analysis, and registration validation via export databases (such as MiCoR).

#### 6. Global Screening and Export Control Pillars
For exporters dealing with dual-use goods or global sales:
* Razorpay’s compliance review identifies the **5 Pillars of Export Compliance**: ECCN Classification, Denied Party Screening, Proper Licensing, End-Use/End-User Verification, and Thorough Recordkeeping.
* Requires compliance with global frameworks (EAR, ITAR, and India's SCOMET list) and inclusion of Destination Control Statements on documentation.

### Points of Consensus

1. **IEC is Non-Negotiable:** All sources agree that an Importer-Exporter Code issued by DGFT is the baseline mandatory document for physical exports; without it, customs clearance and forex banking are blocked.
2. **Harmonized Trade Documents:** There is uniform agreement across logistics, banking, and government sources that descriptions, HS classifications, item quantities, and values must match identically across the Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Shipping Bill.
3. **FEMA / Banking Oversight:** All sources examining finance emphasize that export compliance extends past physical border crossing; it requires complete inward remittance accounting and banking clearance (e-BRC/FIRC).

---

### Points of Contradiction or Ambiguity

* **IEC Requirements for Services:** One source indicates that any business earning foreign currency through trade *or services* must obtain an IEC, whereas another official guidebook explicitly notes that an IEC is not mandatory for service exporters unless they are applying for export promotion incentives under the FTP.
* **Courier E-Commerce Limits & Timelines:** Mentions of regulatory ease slated for "April 1, 2026" appear in business news headlines without fully detailing whether courier export thresholds will rise past ₹10 lakh or what specific FEMA norms will change at that time.
