### Direct Answer to the Search Query

The search query focuses on NITI Aayog’s report (*"Boosting Exports from MSMEs"*, published March 2024) and related metrics concerning Indian MSME export participation, overall export share, and cross-border e-commerce total addressable market (TAM):

*   **MSME Export Participation (~1% vs. 1.1% query):** Sources cite NITI Aayog stating that only **0.95% to ~1% of Indian MSMEs** actively engage in exporting (e.g., out of roughly 1.58 crore to 6.4 crore total MSMEs).
*   **MSME Contribution to National Exports (45–50% share):** MSMEs contribute roughly **43% to 46%** (often rounded to 45–50%) of India’s overall merchandise exports, despite representing only 27% to 29% of national GDP.
*   **Cross-Border E-commerce Size & Market Opportunity (TAM):**
    *   **Current Indian E-commerce Exports:** NITI Aayog (citing GTRI data for 2022) notes that India’s e-commerce exports stand at **barely USD 2 billion**, representing only **0.5%** of total merchandise exports and **0.25%** of global cross-border e-commerce trade.
    *   **The China Comparison:** In contrast, Chinese MSMEs export over **USD 200 billion** via e-commerce platforms (100x India’s volume).
    *   **Global Addressable Export Market for MSME Sectors:** NITI Aayog identifies a global market exceeding **USD 340 billion** across high-potential Indian MSME categories (handicrafts, handloom textiles, ayurvedic/herbal supplements, leather goods, imitation jewellery, wooden products).

### What Each Cluster of Sources Actually Says

#### 1. Official Government & Think Tank Publications (NITI Aayog, Department of Commerce)
*   **Macro Economic Footprint:** The sector consists of roughly 6.3–6.4 crore MSMEs (with ~1.5 crore to 1.58 crore registered on the Udyam portal), generating 11 crore to 28.7 crore jobs, contributing 27% to 29% of GDP, and driving 43% to 46% of total merchandise exports.
*   **The Export Participation Paradox:** Despite accounting for nearly half of exports by volume/value, fewer than 1% (0.95%) of MSMEs export directly.
*   **Bottlenecks & E-commerce Strategy:** Market access and cumbersome compliance (especially payment reconciliation and export procedures) hinder small firms. NITI Aayog recommends simplified e-commerce compliance, "green channel" clearances for MSME exporters, and leveraging digital trade platforms (such as GeM and ONDC) to integrate with global value chains.

### Points of Agreement Across Sources

1.  **Massive Underpenetration of Exporters:** All sources agree that while MSMEs account for almost half of India's merchandise export value (~43–46%), less than 1% (0.95%) of Indian MSMEs actually export directly.
2.  **The India-China E-commerce Gap:** Multiple sources cite the GTRI metric showing India's cross-border e-commerce export figure ($2B) lagging dramatically behind China's ($200B).
3.  **Core Structural Bottlenecks:** Consensus points to burdensome regulatory compliance, payment realization/reconciliation frictions, and customs clearances as the primary inhibitors for small businesses entering global trade.
4.  **Strategic Focus on E-Commerce:** Agreement that cross-border e-commerce platforms and digital public infrastructure (UPI, ONDC, GeM) are the fastest viable levers to scale small merchant exports.

---

### Points of Disagreement and Contradictions

1.  **Discrepancy in Market Sizing (USD 2 Billion vs. USD 48 Million):**
    *   NITI Aayog and GTRI report India’s e-commerce exports at **USD 2 billion** (representing ~0.5% of total merchandise exports).
    *   Conversely, IMARC Group sizes the entire "India cross-border e-commerce market" at **USD 48.00 million in 2025** (reaching USD 207.96 million by 2034).
    *   *Probable cause:* IMARC may be measuring only specific inbound/outbound B2C retail flows, domestic software-enabled transaction values, or an extremely narrow category, whereas GTRI/NITI Aayog measures gross outbound merchandise value exported via e-commerce platforms.
2.  **Employment Numbers:**
    *   NITI Aayog's Senior Advisor message cites MSMEs accounting for **over 11 crore jobs** (employing ~23% of India's workforce).
    *   A secondary NITI Aayog-supported study (*Achieving Efficiencies in MSME Sector Through Convergence of Schemes*) quotes MSMEs generating employment for **over 28.7 crore individuals**.
3.  **GDP and Export Share Variance:**
    *   GDP share is alternately stated as **27%** and **29%**.
    *   MSME export contribution is cited across sources as **43%**, **45%**, and **46%**.
