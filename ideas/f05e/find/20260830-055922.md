## Summary

Here is a summary of the export process, documentation, and compliance requirements for shipping from India to US Amazon FBA based on a surface scan of current search results:

### Core Export Process & Requirements

**1. Indian Export Clearance & Documentation**
To legally export goods from India, sellers generally require:
*   **Importer-Exporter Code (IEC):** Issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT).
*   **Business Registration & Tax IDs:** PAN, GST registration, and business KYC (passport/Aadhaar of founders).
*   **Customs & Banking Documents:** Commercial invoice cum packing list, Letter of Undertaking (LUT) for zero-rated GST export, Authorised Dealer (AD) code, and certificates of origin.
*   **Post-Export Compliance:** Remittance documents such as Foreign Inward Remittance Advice (FIRA) and electronic Bank Realisation Certificates (eBRC) for EDPMS compliance.

**2. US Import Clearance & The "Importer of Record" (IOR)**
*   **Amazon’s Role:** Amazon **will not** act as the Importer of Record (IOR) or customs broker for FBA shipments. Listing Amazon as the IOR on customs paperwork leads to immediate refusal and return of goods at the seller's expense.
*   **IOR Requirement:** Shipments must have a designated US IOR (either a US entity, a foreign importer registered with CBP using a customs broker, or a third-party IOR service) with "Right to Make Entry" and a valid US customs bond. 
*   **Standard Shipping Model:** Many international sellers rely on door-to-door Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) freight forwarders or customs brokers who bundle IOR and clearance services.

**3. US Regulatory & Category-Specific Compliance**
*   **Correct HS/HTS Codes:** Mandatory for US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) clearance and duty calculation.
*   **Regulatory Approvals:** Depending on category, products must comply with agencies like the FDA (food, cosmetics, medical devices), CPSC (children’s goods, toys), or safety certifications such as UL (electronics).

---

### Gaps and Inconsistencies in the Evidence

*   **De Minimis / Section 321 vs. Formal Entry:** One source references the "recent removal of de minimis exemptions," while others mention using Section 321 entries for FBA. The current, practical threshold enforcement for bulk Amazon FBA ocean/air freight vs. individual parcel imports remains ambiguous in these snippets and warrants direct verification with a customs broker.
*   **Foreign Importer Setup vs. Third-Party IOR:** While third-party IOR services heavily advertise their necessity, the data is thin on whether Indian-registered entities can easily register as non-resident importers directly with US Customs without paying expensive intermediary fees.
*   **Platform Guidance Limits:** Amazon provides an "Exports Compliance Dashboard" and "Export Navigator," but sources agree that Amazon offloads the actual legal and clearance liability entirely onto the merchant and third-party logistics providers.
